# Operating Agreement — Levee Line Research LLC

**Single-Member LLC | Louisiana**

---

> **Why a single-member LLC still needs an Operating Agreement:** Louisiana doesn't require filing one with the state, but having a signed OA is the single best protection against "veil-piercing" — courts holding the owner personally liable for company debts. Banks, lenders, and grant agencies will also ask for it. Sign it, notarize it, keep it with company records.

---

**3.2 Admission of New Members — IMPORTANT CAUTION.**

Admitting any new Member converts the Company to a multi-member LLC. This carries two material consequences that must be reviewed in writing before any admission:

1. **Federal tax classification change.** The Company ceases to be a disregarded entity and becomes a partnership for federal tax purposes by default. Form 1065 must be filed, K-1s issued, and capital accounts maintained. This adds annual compliance cost (~$500–$1,500) and complexity.

2. **SBIR/STTR affiliation review under 13 CFR 121.103.** Any prospective Member who owns or controls another business entity may cause that entity's employees and revenue to be aggregated with the Company's for purposes of SBIR/STTR eligibility (500-employee cap and applicable size standards). Before admitting any such Member, the Member proposing the admission shall:
   - (a) Obtain a written disclosure from the prospective Member of all other business entities they own or control, including headcount, revenue, and ownership percentage
   - (b) Confirm in writing that the proposed admission will not cause the Company to exceed any SBIR/STTR size standard or 500-employee threshold under affiliation rules
   - (c) Consult with SBIR-experienced counsel before executing the admission

This caution is recorded because the original contemplated Members (Nolan and Matthew) own other operating businesses. Their admission as Members is not foreclosed but requires the affiliation analysis above; an alternative pathway is participation as advisors (Article XV) or as convertible-note lenders.

**3.3 Procedure for Admission.** New Members may be admitted only upon written amendment of this Agreement executed by the existing Member(s) and the prospective Member, accompanied by the written disclosures and affiliation analysis described in Section 3.2.

## ARTICLE VI — DISTRIBUTIONS

**6.1 Distributions.** As the sole Member, William Brooks Parker is entitled to all Company profits and distributions. Distributions may be made at any time provided that adequate reserves are maintained for:
- (a) Operating expenses
- (b) Grant compliance obligations (segregated grant funds shall not be distributed except as approved within grant budget categories)
- (c) Tax obligations
- (d) Equipment and research costs

**6.2 Grant Fund Segregation.** Federal and state grant funds shall be tracked in a dedicated accounting class or project code. Such funds may only be expended within the approved grant budget and may not be drawn as personal distributions. Violation of this provision is a violation of the underlying grant agreement and creates personal liability beyond the LLC's limited-liability shield.

## ARTICLE VII — TAX MATTERS

**7.1 Default Federal Tax Classification.** As a single-member LLC, the Company is classified as a **disregarded entity** for federal tax purposes by default. Company income, deductions, and credits are reported on the sole Member's personal federal return (Form 1040, Schedule C).

**7.2 Optional S-Corp Election.** The sole Member may elect to have the Company taxed as an S-corporation by filing IRS Form 2553. Such election is generally beneficial only once net business income exceeds approximately $50,000 annually, at which point the self-employment-tax savings can outweigh the payroll-and-reporting compliance cost. Consult a CPA before making this election.

**7.3 Louisiana State Tax.** The Company is treated for Louisiana income tax purposes consistently with its federal classification.

**7.4 Fiscal Year.** The fiscal year of the Company shall be the calendar year (January 1 through December 31).

**10.1 Company Ownership.** All intellectual property created, developed, or discovered in the course of Company business or using Company resources shall be the sole and exclusive property of the Company. This includes:
- (a) Software, algorithms, and source code
- (b) Machine learning models, trained weights, and training data pipelines
- (c) Research data, datasets, and databases
- (d) Inventions, discoveries, and trade secrets
- (e) Publications, reports, and written works
- (f) Patents, patent applications, and patentable subject matter

**10.3 Prior IP.** Intellectual property created by the sole Member before the Company's formation is the sole Member's individual property unless explicitly contributed to the Company in writing.

## ARTICLE XII — DISSOLUTION

**12.1 Events of Dissolution.** The Company shall be dissolved upon:
- (a) Written election of the sole Member
- (b) Entry of a decree of judicial dissolution under Louisiana law
- (c) Any event making it unlawful for the Company to continue

**12.2 Winding Up.** Upon dissolution, the sole Member shall wind up Company affairs by:
- (a) Completing or properly terminating all active grants and contracts in accordance with their terms
- (b) Collecting all debts owed to the Company
- (c) Paying or providing for all Company debts and obligations
- (d) Filing required final tax returns
- (e) Distributing any remaining assets to the sole Member

**12.3 Articles of Dissolution.** Upon completion of winding up, the sole Member shall file Articles of Dissolution with the Louisiana Secretary of State.

## ARTICLE XV — ADVISORY BOARD (NON-EQUITY)

**15.1 Establishment.** The Company may establish an Advisory Board comprising individuals who provide strategic, technical, agricultural, or business-development counsel. Advisors are **not** Members and hold no equity, voting rights, or claim to Company distributions.

**15.2 Composition.** The sole Member appoints and removes Advisors at the sole Member's discretion.

**15.3 Compensation.** Advisors may be compensated through (a) hourly consulting fees, (b) honoraria, (c) reimbursement of reasonable expenses, or (d) other arrangements documented in a written Advisor Agreement. Equity-based compensation is not permitted under this Article; any equity grant to an Advisor would require admission as a Member under Article III.

**15.4 Advisor Representation in Public Materials.** Advisors may be listed in Company materials, grant proposals, business plans, and management-team narratives. Advisor biographies may be included in grant proposals (including SBIR/STTR proposals) as part of the Company's management or commercialization narrative.
