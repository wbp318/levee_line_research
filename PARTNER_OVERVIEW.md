**Prepared:** 2026-05-11 | **Prepared by:** William Brooks Parker, Sole Member

This document explains what Levee Line Research is, how it makes money, and how potential **advisors** and outside collaborators fit. It is the document to share with prospective advisors, partner farmers, university collaborators, and bank loan officers.

## What This Is

Levee Line Research LLC is an AI agriculture research company forming in Lake Providence, Louisiana (East Carroll Parish). The company builds artificial intelligence tools that help Mississippi Delta row-crop growers make better field-level decisions — detecting crop disease earlier, predicting yields more accurately, optimizing seed/fertilizer/water application using multi-modal sensor and imagery data.

**Why it matters.** Precision-agriculture spending is a >$12B/year market growing >12% annually. The big platforms (Climate FieldView/Bayer, Granular/Corteva) charge >$50K/year and target large corporate operations. Small and mid-size Delta growers — most of the actual Louisiana ag economy — are priced out of those tools. Levee Line targets that gap with affordable, research-backed AI built specifically for Delta row crops.

**Why here, why now.** SBIR/STTR was reauthorized through FY2031 in April 2026. AFRI A1541 DSFAS (Data Science for Food & Ag) is the single tightest federal funding fit for this work — $650K per award, 3–5 year projects. Louisiana stacks $100K (Innovation Retention Grant) and up to 30% R&D tax credit on top. East Carroll Parish is rural and economically distressed — that's a competitive advantage on grant scoring, not a limitation.

### Why single-member, not multi-member

The structure decision (2026-05-11): sole-operator LLC, with advisors instead of additional members. Three reasons:

1. **Simpler taxes.** No Form 1065 partnership return. No K-1s. No capital accounts. Income flows to Schedule C directly. Saves ~$500–$1,500/year in accountant fees and removes a class of governance overhead.

2. **Protects SBIR/STTR eligibility.** Federal SBIR rules under 13 CFR 121.103 aggregate the employees and revenue of all businesses commonly owned by an LLC's members. Any member who owns other operating businesses can therefore push Levee Line over the 500-employee SBIR size standard without anyone realizing it until an award is challenged. As a single-member LLC, that exposure is zero.

3. **Decision speed.** Grant deadlines and university partnership negotiations both reward fast, clean decision-making. No supermajority votes, no operating-agreement amendments to admit new members, no buy-out terms to renegotiate.

## What Advisors Bring (Non-Equity)

Levee Line establishes an Advisory Board under Article XV of the Operating Agreement. Advisors are NOT members. They hold no equity, no voting rights, and no claim to distributions. They contribute:

| Advisor Contribution | Examples |
|----------------------|----------|
| Business-development insight | Pricing, customer acquisition, go-to-market |
| Agricultural domain expertise | Agronomy, grower relationships, on-farm operations |
| Technical guidance | Specific AI/ML/sensor/imaging expertise |
| Network access | Industry intros, customer pilots, investor warm contacts |
| Credibility on grant proposals | Named in the management/commercialization narrative |

Advisors may be compensated through hourly consulting fees, honoraria, or expense reimbursement. Equity-based advisor compensation is not used at Levee Line — that would require admission as a Member and would trigger the affiliation review described in the Operating Agreement.

### How can Nolan or Matthew become members later?

Both currently own other operating businesses. Admitting either as a Member triggers SBIR/STTR affiliation review under 13 CFR 121.103 — their other entities' employees and revenue would be aggregated into Levee Line's size-standard math, potentially disqualifying Levee Line from SBIR eligibility. Options if either wants substantial financial participation:

1. **Convertible debt** — They loan capital to Levee Line under a note that converts to equity only on a specified later event (e.g., post-Phase-II raise). No current ownership; affiliation triggered only at conversion, reviewed at that point.
2. **Post-Phase-II equity round** — After the company has SBIR/STTR Phase II proceeds, the size-standard concerns ease and equity participation can be structured with the benefit of an SBIR-experienced attorney.
3. **Customer or vendor relationships** — Either party's other businesses can buy from or supply Levee Line at arm's length without governance complications.

## Why This Works

1. **Almost zero startup cost.** ~$200 to launch. Grants are non-dilutive — no equity given up.

2. **Louisiana rewards federal-grant winners.** IRG ($100K) on top of every SBIR. R&D Tax Credit (5–30%) on top of that. Both stack.

3. **East Carroll Parish is a competitive advantage.** Federal grant reviewers actively weight rural/economically distressed siting in scoring. Most ag-tech startups cluster in Austin or San Francisco; East Carroll applications stand out.

4. **The federal funding direction matches the work.** SBIR/STTR was reauthorized through 2031 in April 2026. AFRI A1541 (Data Science for Food & Ag) is exactly what Levee Line does. USDA-DARPA Food Security MOU (Feb 2026) opened a new ag-security funding lane.

5. **Multiple shots on goal.** NSF SBIR, USDA NIFA SBIR, AFRI A1541, NASA SBIR Appendix B, DARPA FS2, NRCS CIG — separate programs, separate review pools. Missing one doesn't end the year.

6. **All IP belongs to the Company.** Whether the company is acquired, licenses to ag-tech buyers, sells subscriptions to growers, or simply accumulates value as a research IP portfolio — the upside accrues to a single owner.
