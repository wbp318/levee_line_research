## SECTION A — Pull Your Credit Reports and Dispute Errors

### Why this matters

Errors on credit reports are extremely common. Removing a single bad item — wrong account, expired collection, identity mix-up — can lift your FICO 40–60 points. This costs nothing and is the highest-ROI credit move you can make. Even if you never apply for a loan, you want clean reports for any business relationship that does a credit check.

### Step 1: Pull all three reports

**The ONLY legitimate free source:** https://www.annualcreditreport.com

This is the site authorized by federal law — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion are required to give you one free report per year through this site. Don't use any other site that asks for a credit card "for verification."

1. Go to annualcreditreport.com
2. Click "Request your free credit reports"
3. Fill in name, address, DOB, SSN
4. Choose all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) — get them all at once or stagger every 4 months for ongoing monitoring
5. Answer identity verification questions (will ask about past addresses, accounts, loan amounts — some of these are intentionally wrong to catch identity thieves; pick the most accurate answer)
6. Download/print each report

If you fail identity verification (happens sometimes after moves), you can request by phone (1-877-322-8228) or mail.

### Step 2: Read each report carefully

Look for these red flags:

| Red Flag | What to Do |
|---|---|
| **Accounts you don't recognize** | Possible identity theft — dispute and report at identitytheft.gov |
| **Wrong balances** (showing more than you owe) | Dispute |
| **Wrong account status** (showing open when closed; showing late when paid on time) | Dispute |
| **Old debts past the 7-year reporting window** (most negatives must drop off after 7 years; bankruptcies after 7–10 years) | Dispute |
| **Hard inquiries you didn't authorize** | Dispute |
| **Wrong personal info** (wrong address, wrong name spelling, wrong employer) | Dispute |
| **Same debt listed multiple times** (same collection re-aged or re-listed under different collector) | Dispute |
| **Medical debt under $500** (as of 2023, medical collections under $500 should not appear) | Dispute if you see any |

### Step 3: Dispute errors

Each bureau has an online dispute tool — that's the fastest. By federal law (FCRA), they have **30 days** to investigate or remove the item.

**Online dispute links:**
- Equifax: https://www.equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-dispute/
- Experian: https://www.experian.com/disputes/main.html
- TransUnion: https://www.transunion.com/credit-disputes/dispute-your-credit

**If disputing by mail (sometimes more effective for complex disputes):**

### Step 4: After dispute resolution

- If item is removed or corrected → save the updated report; your FICO should reflect this within 30–45 days
- If item is verified (bureau says it's accurate) → you can add a 100-word consumer statement to your file explaining your side
- Pull a new free report at annualcreditreport.com 30 days later to confirm changes

### What NOT to do

- **Don't pay for "credit repair services"** — most are scams and they can only do what you can do yourself for free
- **Don't pay for FICO scores** — your bank, your credit cards, and apps like Credit Karma show you scores for free (Credit Karma uses VantageScore, which is close enough for tracking)
- **Don't apply for new credit while disputing** — hard inquiries hurt your score for 12 months
- **Don't close old credit cards** — average age of accounts is part of your score; keeping old cards open helps

## SECTION C — Your One-Paragraph Problem Statement

This is the single most important piece of writing for your grants. Every reviewer reads it. Every advisor will ask for it. Every grower you approach for a letter of support will want it.

### What it has to do

Cover four things in one paragraph (3–5 sentences):

1. **The problem** — a specific pain in Delta row-crop agriculture
2. **The gap** — why existing tools don't solve it
3. **Your approach** — what Levee Line will do, in plain language
4. **Why it matters** — who benefits and how

### Draft you can use as a starting point

```
Small and mid-size row-crop growers in the Mississippi Delta — operations
under 5,000 acres growing cotton, soybeans, corn, and rice — have no
affordable, interpretable AI tools for predicting yield gaps at the field
level. Commercial precision-agriculture platforms cost $50,000 a year or
more and are built for large corporate operations; small growers either
go without or rely on generic county-level forecasts that don't reflect
their specific soil, irrigation, and management conditions. Levee Line
Research is developing an AI system that fuses UAV multispectral and
thermal imagery, Sentinel-2 satellite data, and low-cost ground sensors
to produce field-level yield-gap predictions with farmer-validated
interpretability — built specifically for Delta growing conditions and
priced for the operations the major platforms ignore. The result is
better-informed planting, input, and harvest decisions for the growers
who power East Carroll Parish, the Louisiana Delta, and the broader
Mississippi Delta agricultural economy.
```

### How to make it yours

- **Replace generalities with specifics from your own experience.** If you grew up on a soybean operation, name it. If you know specific growers whose yield variability you've watched, ground the language in that.
- **Pick ONE crop to lead with.** Reviewers prefer focus. Cotton, soybeans, corn, or rice — pick whichever your starter dataset and grower contacts skew toward.
- **Read it out loud.** If it sounds like a textbook, it's wrong. If it sounds like you actually care, it's right.
- **Iterate.** First draft will be bad. That's fine. Rewrite it three times across a week.

### Variant: the one-sentence "elevator" version

For LinkedIn bio, emails, casual intros:

```
Levee Line Research builds affordable, interpretable AI tools that help
small and mid-size Mississippi Delta row-crop growers predict yield gaps
at the field level — the operations commercial precision-ag platforms
ignore.
```

### How to fill this in if you feel like you have "nothing to put"

You probably have more than you think. Categories to mine:

| Category | What counts |
|---|---|
| **Formal education** | Any degree, any field. Even an associate's. |
| **Self-taught technical skills** | Python, ML libraries, GIS, drone operation — list them honestly |
| **Work experience** | Every job that involved problem-solving, data, technology, or agriculture |
| **Volunteer/community work** | Especially if it involved organizing, leading, or building something |
| **Personal projects** | A model you trained, a dataset you cleaned, a piece of software you wrote, a paper you wrote in school |
| **Industry knowledge** | If you grew up around farming, that's domain expertise — frame it accurately |
| **Open-source contributions** | A pull request, an issue you filed, a blog post you wrote |
| **Self-published writing** | Technical blog posts, Medium articles, technical Twitter threads with substantive content |

The biosketch doesn't require a PhD. It requires HONEST capability. Reviewers can smell faked expertise — they cannot smell self-taught, well-applied real capability. Lead with what you actually know.

## SECTION E — The Cross-Border Play (Why UADA Rohwer & UAPB Matter)

The two AR-side university emails in Section B aren't optional networking. They're the highest-ROI move you can make to access Arkansas funding without relocating. Here's the logic in one place — full detail in `louisiana/13_AR_CROSS_BORDER.md`.

### The core idea

Most Arkansas programs gate on **where the work is performed**, not where the company is based. By signing a research subcontract with an Arkansas university, your contracted research dollars qualify for the **Arkansas University-Based R&D Tax Credit (33%, no statewide cap published)** even though Levee Line stays domiciled in Louisiana.

That's it. One contract = a 33% credit on every dollar paid to the AR university under that contract, plus any of your own AR-incurred QREs.

### Numbers, plain

A typical first-year cross-border setup looks like this:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Research subcontract with UADA Rohwer (12 months, modest scope) | $50,000 |
| AR University-Based R&D Tax Credit on that contract | $16,500 (33%) |
| Your AR-side QREs on the joint work (travel, field tools, contractor time) | $10,000 |
| Additional AR credit on those QREs | $3,300 |
| **Effective AR funding via the credit alone** | **~$19,800 / year** |

Stack that with the **Louisiana** R&D Tax Credit (5–30% on your LA-incurred QREs — the SAME work just done in Lake Providence) and you've got two complementary state credit streams from a single research program.

### What unlocks which program

| Program | What you need to access it |
|---|---|
| **AR University-Based R&D Tax Credit (33%)** | A signed research contract with an AR university. That's it. |
| **ARA Impact Grants ($75K, industry partner role)** | The PI must be at an AR partner institution; you supply industry match. Builds naturally on an existing UADA or UAPB relationship. |
| **UAPB 1890 Capacity Building Grants ($100K–$750K subaward)** | UAPB primes the proposal; you're listed as industry subrecipient on AI/ML components. Builds naturally on the UAPB ORSP email. |

### What you DON'T need to do

- **Don't foreign-LLC-register in Arkansas yet.** Adds compliance overhead without payoff at this stage.
- **Don't open an AR business location.** Required only for Advantage Arkansas / Targeted Business Payroll Credit — those are off the table without relocating, and that's fine.
- **Don't relocate.** That decision was reversed 2026-05-11 (see `RULES.md` Section 1.3).

Because the cross-border work starts with **two emails you can send this week for $0** — Email 2 (UADA Rohwer / Linda Martin) and Email 3 (UAPB ORSP) in Section B. Send those emails. The 33% credit conversation starts with an introduction; the contract itself comes months later, well after the LLC is formed and funded.
