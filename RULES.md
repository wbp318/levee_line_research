## 2. Membership Admission — SBIR Affiliation Hazard

**2.1 Default: keep prospective contributors as Advisors, not Members.** Article XV of the Operating Agreement establishes a non-equity Advisory Board for exactly this purpose. Advisors can appear in grant proposal management narratives, contribute via paid consulting, and receive credit without any of the ownership consequences.

**2.2 Specifically: Nolan and Matthew.** Both own other operating businesses. Admitting either as a Member of Levee Line triggers affiliation review under **13 CFR 121.103**. The SBA aggregates the employees and revenue of all businesses commonly owned by an LLC's members for purposes of SBIR/STTR eligibility (500-employee cap, applicable size standards). If their other entities have material headcount or revenue, admitting them as Members can disqualify Levee Line from SBIR awards without anyone noticing until an award is challenged. Default answer: advisors, not members.

**2.3 If they want to contribute capital,** structure as:
- **Convertible debt** — loan now, conversion to equity only on a defined later event (e.g., post-Phase-II raise). Affiliation reviewed at conversion, not now.
- **Customer or vendor relationships** — their other businesses buy from or supply Levee Line at arm's length.
- **Post-Phase-II equity round** — after Phase II proceeds land, the size-standard concerns ease and equity participation can be structured with an SBIR-experienced attorney.

**2.4 Pre-admission checklist (required before admitting any new Member).** Document in writing:
- All other business entities the prospective Member owns or controls (name, headcount, annual revenue, ownership %)
- A written affiliation analysis confirming Levee Line will remain under all applicable SBIR/STTR size standards after admission
- Consultation with SBIR-experienced counsel — not "we read the regs ourselves"

## 3. Scope Discipline — One Entity, One Mission

**3.1 The Company's purpose is agricultural R&D.** NAICS 541715. Stated in the Articles of Organization and the Operating Agreement.

**3.2 Do not run non-agriculture revenue through Levee Line.** That includes — explicitly — GTA VI release prep, ecommerce, marketing services, consulting outside the ag-research scope, content production, and any other side ventures.

**3.3 Why co-mingling is dangerous.**
- **LA R&D Tax Credit risk.** QREs must align with the entity's stated research mission. Non-ag revenue dilutes the QRE basis and invites a "this is not a research company" challenge from Louisiana Department of Revenue.
- **Federal grant compliance risk.** SBIR/USDA grant funds and grant-funded researcher time must stay within the proposed scope. Co-mingled accounting on cross-business activity is an audit flag.
- **SBA size-standard risk.** SBIR eligibility tests headcount, revenue, and affiliation across all activity inside the entity. Off-topic revenue inflates the size math.

**3.4 Where non-Levee-Line work goes:**
- **318ecom LLC** — ecommerce, marketing-adjacent revenue, anything that fits its existing scope
- **A new separate LLC** — any side venture meaningful enough to deserve its own entity
- **Schedule C as a sole proprietor on the personal 1040** — ad-hoc gigs not big enough to justify an entity

## 4. Grant-Funded Activity — Compliance Hygiene

**4.1 Segregated accounting.** Every federal or state grant gets its own class / project code in QuickBooks (or equivalent). Grant cash flows through the business bank account but is tracked separately. Distributions and personal owner draws never come from grant funds.

**4.2 Time-and-effort tracking.** When grant work begins, log hours by project. 2 CFR 200 (Uniform Guidance) requires auditable time charging.

**4.3 Records retained 7 years.** Bank statements, receipts, contracts, payroll (if/when applicable), grant award letters, expenditure reports, progress reports, audit documentation.

**4.4 No grant funds drawn as owner distributions.** Even though you're the sole Member, federal grant rules apply. Pay yourself a salary or owner draw only from non-grant Company cash, or from grant-budget line items specifically approved for principal-investigator salary.

**4.5 Data Management Plan (DMP) required.** Every USDA NIFA competitive grant (since FY2019) and most federal research grants require a DMP. Template lives in `research/09_AG_RESEARCH_SCOPE.md`.

## 5. Quantified-Research-Expenditures (QRE) Logging

**5.1 Track from Day 1.** Date, vendor, amount, category, location (LA or AR). Categories: Computing, Equipment, Software, Data, Travel, Contractors, Supplies, Wages.

**5.2 LA QREs vs AR QREs separated.** The LA R&D Tax Credit applies to LA-incurred QREs (5–30%). The AR University-Based R&D Tax Credit applies to AR-incurred QREs under a research contract with an AR university (33%). The same dollar cannot count for both.

**5.3 Apply early.** LA's $12M annual cap is first-come, first-served. File 2026-07-01 day one of the FY26-27 allocation window.

## 7. AI Tools in Grant Writing — Use, Don't Lean

**7.1 No federal grant reviewer runs your proposal through an AI detector.** Detection tools are unreliable and most agencies haven't authorized their use. NSF (June 2024 policy) actually prohibits *reviewers* from running your proposal through generative AI — the policy risk runs the other way.

**7.2 Plagiarism software is real.** NIH and some agencies use iThenticate. It scans for copy-paste against published literature and prior proposals. Different from AI detection. If you cite, cite. If you paraphrase, paraphrase. Don't lift from your own prior work without rewriting or self-citing.

**7.3 Disclose substantive AI use when asked.** Read each Funding Opportunity Notice. NIH issued draft guidance in 2024; some specific solicitations now require disclosure. Using AI as an editing tool is fine; using it to write your Innovation/Significance/Specific-Aims sections wholesale and not disclosing is the kind of thing that backfires.

**7.4 The real risk is "AI slop" reviewers can smell.** Generic openings, padded transitions, vague claims without specific data — these read as machine-generated and drag your score down even if no detector is ever used.

**7.5 Use AI for:**
- Outlining, structuring, brainstorming approaches
- Editing for clarity and flow
- Drafting boilerplate (DMP, biosketch formatting)
- Summarizing literature you've actually read

**7.6 Don't use AI for:**
- Writing Specific Aims from scratch
- Generating Significance/Innovation narratives
- Anything you can't explain back to a reviewer in conversation
- "Inventing" preliminary results or citations (hallucinations are caught fast)

**7.7 Spend the LTTO $5K on a human grant writer.** The Louisiana LTTO proposal-prep reimbursement is designed for exactly this — pay a human freelancer or grant consultant for the polish layer. They catch what reviewers smell.
